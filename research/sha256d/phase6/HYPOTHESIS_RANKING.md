## Method

Seven factors, each 0 to 5. Score = 20 x (0.25 S +
0.20 P + 0.15 C + 0.10 V + 0.15 R + 0.05 D + 0.10 X),
range 0 to 100.

- S, potential speedup: read from the registry's own
  Impact statement (negligible 0.5, small 1.5,
  small-to-moderate 2.5, moderate 3.5,
  moderate-to-large 4.5, breakthrough-class claim 5,
  indirect or methodology 1). S measures the size of
  the claim, never its credibility; credibility is
  P's job, so a large implausible claim cannot win
  on S alone.
- P, mathematical plausibility: from the registry's
  Confidence (high 5, medium 3, low 1).
- C, implementation cost inverted (low 5, medium 3,
  high 1), from the registry's Cost.
- V, verification difficulty inverted (Difficulty
  low 5, medium 3.5, high 2, very high 1).
- R, expected research value: what the programme
  learns whether the hypothesis wins or loses.
  Methodology multipliers and graph-changing
  questions score high; one-off tweaks score low.
- D, dependency readiness: 5 means testable now with
  existing code and data; lower when the entry waits
  on an unbuilt predecessor or an environmental
  event (a GPU, a second socket).
- X, duplication risk inverted: 5 means clearly new
  ground; lower for near-repeats of tested ideas,
  even when a new element is stated.

Evidence overrides applied in this run (all dated
2026-11-14): Y-065 P = 0 and Y-075 P = 0, because
EXP-016 refuted both as work reductions after this
ranking's source registry was written; their
research value is carried in R (both were worth
closing properly). Y-090 D = 1, because its
mechanism is conditional on Y-065 or Y-075 and both
are now closed. Two S nuances: Y-005 scored 2.0
(its Impact text is a speculative large claim with
measured support against it) and Y-036 scored 3.0
(its moderate claim is density-conditional).

How to read the result: the method deliberately
rewards decidable, cheap, high-information
experiments, because the audit
(PHASE6_RESEARCH_PLAN.md section 1.5) found the
programme's binding constraint is unsettled
decisions, not a shortage of ideas. Scores within
about 1.5 points of each other are a tie band under
this method, not a meaningful order: ranks 7 to 12
all sit at 74.5. The ranking orders work; it does
not grade truth, and a low score for a Workstream B
entry (Y-047, rank 105) means "expensive to settle
relative to what it teaches this programme now",
not "false".

## The ten highest-priority candidates

1. **Y-103, equal-pair control methodology (78.5).**
   Run one kernel under two names through the full
   decision procedure; it must return a null. This
   is the harness certifying itself. Until a control
   has produced a decision-grade null, every
   positive decision the procedure returns is
   suspect. Selected inside EXP-017.
2. **Y-097, heterogeneous worker pair under the VM
   cap (78.0).** One MB worker plus one SHA-NI
   stream worker, against both homogeneous pairs.
   Cheap, one session, and it aims at a measured
   anomaly (EXP-007's flat vector response while
   scalar rates rose) that suggests the two families
   contend for different resources. If the mixed
   pair beats both homogeneous pairs, the deployment
   shape of every future kernel decision changes.
3. **Y-102, fine-grained paired interleaving
   (77.5).** ABBA blocks of 100k hashes with
   block-level paired inference, replacing the
   per-round medians whose spreads EXP-010
   measured at 115 to 208 percent. Selected
   inside EXP-017.
4. **Y-108, pinned benchmark manifest (77.0).**
   Every record carries binary hashes, source
   revisions, flags, environment and covariates, so
   cross-session comparisons stop resting on
   transcriptions. Adopted as a standing rule this
   phase; its rank reflects that it multiplies the
   value of everything ranked below it.
5. **Y-101, steal-normalised decision statistic
   (76.0).** Regress candidate-minus-control on the
   steal delta, validated by injecting known steal.
   EXP-010 found the raw correlation weak for the
   vector kernel, which is exactly why the statistic
   needs a validation experiment rather than another
   assumption. Selected inside EXP-017.
6. **Y-084, flags-only MB batch via mask registers
   (75.5).** Search workloads need flags, not 32-byte
   digests; mask registers can carry the comparison
   result without the digest store stream. High
   confidence, moderate claim, and it composes with
   whichever MB kernel survives EXP-018.
7. **Y-002, four-input schedule addition via one
   carry-save level, costed per distance (74.5, tie
   band).** The input experiment to Y-001: price the
   representation before building the engine.
   Selected as the first half of EXP-019.
8. **Y-076, extranonce-dimension midstate
   amortisation in the solo shape (74.5, tie band).**
   The solo server's real workload varies extranonce
   as well as nonce; the reuse structure across that
   dimension is cheap to derive and directly
   production-shaped (the lab's own node is syncing
   toward exactly this deployment).
9. **Y-077, two-level midstate cache for job
   families (74.5, tie band).** Companion to Y-076:
   job families share prefixes above the block-2
   midstate; a two-level cache is the pipeline form
   of the same observation.
10. **Y-079, snapshot-depth decision table by
    template field (74.5, tie band).** Which template
    fields justify which snapshot depth is currently
    folklore; a decision table makes the pipeline's
    precomputation choices explicit and auditable.

Ranks 11 and 12 (Y-096, MB champion plus flags-only
batch at integration shape; Y-105, confirmation
path repricing at MB rates) sit in the same 74.5
tie band and are the natural next pair once EXP-017
and EXP-018 land.

## Workstream B: the fifteen named hypotheses

Each entry: its rank under this method, the precise
unresolved question, and the designed experiment.
Ranking position does not gate Workstream B; the
phase directive names these fifteen for explicit
treatment wherever they land.

### Y-001, carry-save redundant arithmetic for schedule generation (rank 35, 68.5)

Unresolved question: across the schedule recurrence
W_t = s1(W_{t-2}) + W_{t-7} + s0(W_{t-15}) + W_{t-16},
at which distances does deferring carry propagation
in a redundant (sum, carry) representation actually
reduce the critical path or the op count, once the
mandatory resolution points (feeding s1, feeding
the round's T1) are priced in?
Designed experiment: EXP-019, second half. Build
the schedule generator with carry-save state per
distance class, on top of Y-002's per-distance
price list; differential-test every generated word
against the reference schedule for 1M random
templates; report the price list with and without
resolution costs separated. Success: at least one
distance class with a measured saving that survives
resolution pricing. EXP-016 does not bear on this
entry: it refuted nonce separation, not redundant
representation.

### Y-014, full-round algebraic rewriting (rank 77, 62.5)

Unresolved question: does the joint Boolean and
arithmetic cone of one full round (T1, T2, the
register update) admit an equivalent network with
fewer primitive operations than the conventional
form, in the instruction sets this machine offers?
The cone results (EXP-012) settle the Boolean part
in isolation only.
Designed experiment: EXP-020. Equality saturation
over the round cone with the rewrite set of
COMPUTATION_GRAPH_RESEARCH.md, z3 proof per
candidate, assembly audit, decision under EXP-017's
statistic. Its low rank is a cost statement (the
machinery is the cost), not a plausibility
statement; it is selected anyway, because its
research value dominates the ranking's cost term.

### Y-015, SAT-guided expression rewriting (rank 24, 70.5)

Unresolved question: can SAT/SMT-guided rewriting
find equivalent forms that equality saturation's
fixed rewrite set cannot reach, in bounded time,
for the T1 and T2 cones?
Designed experiment: inside EXP-020 as the second
engine on identical cones and identical proof
gates, so the two engines' yields are comparable.
Success is comparative: any proven candidate the
saturation engine missed, or a bounded-time null
that prices the approach.

### Y-023, register-resident dual-batch MB (rank 14, 74.0)

Unresolved question: can the dual-batch shape hold
all state in the 32 zmm registers across a full
unrolled body (spill count zero for state), and if
it can, does EXP-011's session-A rate become
reproducible under a decision-grade statistic?
Designed experiment: EXP-018. Assembly-first:
the spill audit is reported before any rate; the
rate is decided under EXP-017's statistic with the
Y-108 manifest. Failure is also a result: a proof
by construction that the register file forbids the
shape at this unroll depth redirects Workstream D
to Y-024 (single-batch register-resident) with
evidence instead of taste.

### Y-025, schedule generation distance in MB, eight-lane variant (rank 78, 62.5)

Unresolved question: for the eight-lane geometry,
what schedule lead distance minimises the sum of
schedule latency exposure and register pressure?
EXP-005 mapped distance for the SHA-NI stream
geometries; the vector family's optimum is unmapped
and EXP-019's price list changes the costs it must
be computed from.
Designed experiment: after EXP-019, a distance
sweep (lead 2, 4, 6, 8 rounds) in the EXP-018
walker shape, decided under EXP-017's statistic,
reported as a curve, not a winner.

### Y-047, bounded superoptimiser for one SHA-NI round pair (rank 105, 46.5)

Unresolved question: within a bounded instruction
budget, does any sequence compute two consecutive
SHA-NI rounds (as RNDS2 pairs plus their operand
preparation) in fewer cycles than the incumbent
sequence, on this core?
Designed experiment: deferred by selection, not by
verdict. EXP-020's superoptimisation component is
built at vector width first; if its search space
proves intractable there, Y-047 becomes the bounded
fallback with the same proof gate. Its rank is the
method working as intended: high cost, narrow
teaching, real duplication risk against EXP-020.
It is recorded here so the deferral is a decision,
not an oversight.

### Y-048, alternative multi-buffer arithmetic formulation 1 (rank 69, 63.5)

Unresolved question: does the first registered
alternative formulation of the MB round arithmetic
(the addition-network variant) reduce the vector
op census of set C below 240 per hash at equal
correctness?
Designed experiment: enter EXP-020's pipeline as a
seeded candidate family: formalise the formulation
as rewrite rules plus a hand candidate, prove with
z3, census by static count, and only then measure.
Standalone benchmarking before the census and
proof would repeat the programme's old mistake of
pricing unproven forms.

### Y-049, alternative multi-buffer arithmetic formulation 2 (rank 96, 54.5)

Unresolved question: same form as Y-048 for the
second registered formulation, which additionally
restructures operand preparation: does the saving
survive the preparation it moves, the exact failure
mode of EXP-014's fold?
Designed experiment: as Y-048, with the additional
requirement that preparation cost is reported
inside the candidate's census, not beside it
(the EXP-014 lesson, made a gate).

### Y-056, vector schedule just-in-time distance tuning (rank 21, 71.5)

Unresolved question: EXP-005 found the stream
geometry flat in streams; is the MB schedule's
just-in-time distance similarly flat, or does the
vector family have a sharp optimum the current
fixed distance misses?
Designed experiment: a distance sweep inside the
EXP-007 kernel (the simplest MB walker), under
EXP-017's statistic, reported as a curve with
register counts per point. Cheap once EXP-017
exists; meaningless before it, which is why it is
designed but not selected in the first five.

### Y-065, nonce-affine schedule decomposition (rank 83, 61.0)

Unresolved question: ANSWERED by EXP-016. Whether
W_t = C_t + L_t(nonce) with cheap L_t exists beyond
the trivial words: it exists exactly for W18
(L = sigma0) and W19 (L = identity), saves nothing
at either, and is refuted from W20 by proof and
measurement (MATHEMATICAL_DISCOVERIES.md MD-1).
Designed experiment: none further; the derivation
was the experiment. Its score keeps a high R
because closing a breakthrough-tier claim with
proof grade evidence is valuable, and a P of 0
because the claim itself is dead. Any future
nonce-separation proposal must state which step of
MD-1 it contradicts.

### Y-075, round-3 state delta decomposition (rank 81, 62.0)

Unresolved question: ANSWERED by EXP-016. Whether a
(base, delta) state form propagates past round 3
with a cheap delta: the form is exact at S_4 only,
round 4's fresh words are already nonlinear in the
nonce, and the affine words at S_5 to S_7 are
unmodified register copies (MD-1). Designed
experiment: none further. As with Y-065, revival
requires contradicting a named step of the
derivation, not restating the hope.

### Y-093, GPU kernel re-audit if hardware appears (rank 28, 70.5)

Unresolved question: none that is testable today.
The hypothesis is conditional on an environmental
event (a GPU device existing). The production GPU
engine reports zero device hashes on this machine
and CUDA returns error 35; there is no benchmark
to design against absent hardware.
Designed experiment: trigger-based. On the first
day a device is visible to the runtime, run the
Task 003.9 accounting suite against gpu/engine.py,
then the FP32-carry-limb versus INT32 comparison
the original commission specified, with device_hashes
reported separately from backend hashes. Until
then this entry is a tripwire, not a task.

### Y-101, steal-normalised decision statistic (rank 5, 76.0)

Unresolved question: does conditioning the
candidate-minus-control difference on the steal
delta actually reduce decision variance, given
EXP-010's weak raw correlations (+0.221 vector,
-0.521 control)? Designed experiment: EXP-017,
first component: inject known steal with a
calibrated spinner, and require the statistic to
recover the injected effect size within its stated
interval before it is trusted on real candidates.

### Y-102, fine-grained paired interleaving (rank 3, 77.5)

Unresolved question: at what block size does paired
interleaving stop tracking the environment (too
large) without starving the kernels' warm-up (too
small)? Designed experiment: EXP-017, second
component: sweep block sizes 10k, 100k, 1M hashes
under the equal-pair control (Y-103); the chosen
size is the smallest whose control runs return
decision-grade nulls across sessions.

### Y-108, pinned benchmark manifest (rank 4, 77.0)

Unresolved question: none open; the question was
whether the programme would adopt it before or
after the next transcription-dependent claim.
Designed experiment: not an experiment, a rule,
adopted this phase: no benchmark record is
programme-grade without binary hashes, source
revision, flags, environment, and covariates
attached. EXP-017 is its first enforcement.
